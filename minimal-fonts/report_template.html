Concavities

Which way the notches open

A notch is a one-pixel-wide slot of paper with ink on both sides and ink closing one end. Its depth is how many blocks long it is. The big number counts notches of the depth you pick; the letters underneath have one.

Handwriting

Pixel strokes against pen strokes

“Pen” columns are pen-down strokes in classroom manuscript: continuous style (one stroke per letter unless the pen must lift) and traditional ball-and-stick (circles and sticks drawn separately). “Pixel” columns are the fewest strokes that draw every pixel link of the glyph without going over a link twice. Orange marks a pixel count that differs from the continuous-style count.

Shape families

Letters that are the same shape turned or flipped

The fewer pixels a font has, the more its letters reuse one another. These are exact matches after trimming to the ink.

Method

How each feature is measured

  • Extraction. The 3×5 PNG is 104 px wide: 26 cells of 3 columns plus a 1-column gap. The 5×5 PNG is 120 px wide with glyphs 1 to 5 columns wide, split on blank columns. Both are 5 rows tall with no baseline offset, so descenders (g, j, p, q, y) sit inside the same 5 rows.
  • Loop. A region of paper that can't reach the outside moving only up, down, left or right. Ink is treated as 8-connected, so a diagonal pixel step still seals a wall.
  • Slant. Two ink pixels that touch only at a corner. If that corner link just joins a horizontal run to a vertical run (the rounded corners of the 5×5 o, the top of s), it's counted as a rounded corner instead.
  • Notch (concavity). Start at a paper cell whose neighbour in one direction is ink. Walk the opposite way while both side cells are ink. The number of cells walked is the depth and the walking direction is where it opens. A “two-block concave” is a notch of depth 2.
  • Pixel strokes. Pixels are graph nodes, linked to their orthogonal neighbours and to diagonal neighbours that touch only at a corner. The stroke count is the Euler-path count: half the number of odd-degree pixels in each connected piece, minimum one per piece. A T-junction therefore costs two strokes, as it does with a pen.
  • Handwriting. Formation cues follow common classroom manuscript charts (for example the W&M T/TAC lowercase formation chart). Programs differ on k and y: some teach k as one stroke and y as a u-shape with a tail. The counts here use the most common versions.
